Spotifie Web Player

Your Music, Your Way

A hybrid music player that brings a published catalogue and the music already on your machine into one library. Your own audio is indexed and played locally — it is never uploaded — while a shared catalogue streams to anyone who opens the app.

Powerful Features

Spotifie runs on a single local Node server that serves the interface, indexes the music on this machine and speaks to the shared catalogue. Two sources of music sit side by side in one library: albums an administrator has published for everyone, and the audio found on this device. Both play through the same player, with the same controls, under stable identifiers that never expose a filesystem path to the browser.

Published Catalogue

Albums an administrator publishes are available to everyone who opens Spotifie, including guests who have not signed in. Artwork and audio resolve through short-lived signed links that are requested when needed and never stored.

Local Music Discovery

Spotifie searches the folders music normally lives in — Music, Downloads, Desktop and Documents — reading tags and artwork into a local index. Everything it finds appears as one Local Music collection. After the first pass it only looks at what has changed, recognising unchanged files by path, size and modified time.

Your Own Collections

Build albums of your own from anything in the library, import audio straight from your device, and keep a Liked Songs collection. A published album can be renamed, re-covered or re-described for you alone — the shared record is never touched, and your version stays on this device.

A Complete Player

Play and pause, previous and next, a seek bar you can drag or reach with the keyboard, volume, shuffle that walks a collection once through, and repeat off, all or one. An expanded Now Playing view opens over the page without interrupting the sound, and where the browser supports it the system's own media controls work too.

Dark & Light, Every Screen

A full light theme alongside the dark one, with contrast checked across both, and a layout built for a phone, a tablet and a desktop rather than scaled down from one of them. Your choice of theme is remembered on this device.

Search, Resume and Backup

Search across everything in the library by album or song. Spotifie remembers where you stopped in each track and starts you there next time. Your collections and preferences can be exported and restored — the file carries what you organised, never the audio itself.

How It Works

Two libraries, one player. Spotifie runs from a single Node server on your own machine. That one process serves the interface, indexes the music already on the device, and reads the catalogue an administrator has published for everyone. Anyone can listen as a guest; signing in adds a profile and keeps your collections and listening positions with your account.

1. Start Spotifie

Start the local server and open it in any modern browser. One process serves the pages, the library API and the audio — on a desktop, a tablet or a phone.

2. Browse the Published Catalogue

Albums an administrator has published are there the moment the page loads, for signed-in listeners and guests alike. Search reaches any album or song across the whole library.

3. Let Spotifie Find Your Music

The first run walks the folders music usually lives in and builds a local index of what it finds. Everything on the device appears as one Local Music collection, listed first. Later runs only look at what changed.

4. Make the Library Yours

Group tracks into albums of your own, import audio straight from the device, keep a Liked Songs collection, and rename or re-cover a published album for yourself without touching the shared copy.

5. Play It, and Pick Up Where You Stopped

One player throughout: shuffle, repeat, seek, volume and an expanded Now Playing view. Spotifie remembers your position in each track, and your collections can be exported and restored at any time.

Your Data

Privacy & Data

Your music stays where it already is. Audio found on this device, and audio you import from it, is indexed and played by the Spotifie server running on your own machine. It is never uploaded to Supabase or anywhere else. Supabase holds your account and profile, and serves the catalogue an administrator has published for everyone — the two stay separate.

Local Audio Stays Local

Files found by the device search, and files you import, are read from disk and streamed by the local server. Only the identifier, tag data and artwork needed to draw the library ever reach the page — the filesystem path never does, and the audio itself is never sent to Supabase.

Where the Search Looks

The search covers the profile folders music normally lives in, the configured music root, and any extra locations the person running the server has configured. System, program and dependency folders are skipped. Folder names are logged on the server only and are never sent to the browser.

Account and Session

Signing in is optional — guests can listen to the published catalogue and to this device's music. If you do sign in, Supabase handles authentication and your profile: your email, display name and the details that come with an account. The session is kept by the Supabase client in your browser's storage. Spotifie sets no cookies of its own and loads no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts.

What Is Kept, and Clearing It

Your theme, player preferences and collections live in this browser's local storage and IndexedDB. The music index, artwork cache and the position you reached in each track live in Spotifie's own working folder beside the server, never in Supabase. Both are rebuildable: clear the site data, or delete that folder, and Spotifie starts over.

Accessibility

Music is for everyone. Accessibility is part of how the interface is built rather than something added at the end, and it is checked again as the application changes. What follows describes what is in place today. It is a statement of practice, not a claim of formal WCAG conformance, and the work continues.

Keyboard Navigation

Every control can be reached and used from the keyboard. Buttons and links are real buttons and links, volume is a native range input, the seek bar is a focusable slider that answers the arrow keys along with Home and End, and menus, dialogs and the library drawer close with Escape.

Labels and Semantics

Icon-only controls carry accessible names, menus and dialogs carry the roles that describe them, and live regions announce scan results and status messages. Each page keeps a sensible heading order and landmark structure for screen readers to follow.

Visible Focus and Contrast

Focus is never invisible: a distinct focus indicator is drawn on controls across the player, header, library and album pages. Both themes are built from semantic colour tokens, with text, icons and control states checked for contrast in light and dark alike.

Scale, Touch and Motion

Layouts and text scale from a large monitor down to a phone, with touch targets sized for a finger rather than a cursor, and hover effects kept to devices that actually hover. When the system asks for reduced motion, animations and transitions are turned off.
